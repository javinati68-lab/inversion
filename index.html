Activity 3: Sentence Inversion

What is Sentence Inversion?

Inversion occurs when we put an auxiliary verb before the subject of a clause. This is commonly used after negative or restrictive adverbial phrases placed at the beginning of a sentence for emphasis or dramatic effect.

  • Standard: I have never seen such a beautiful sunset.
  • Inverted: Never have I seen such a beautiful sunset.

Common triggers: Never, Rarely, Seldom, Hardly, Scarcely, Little, Not only, Under no circumstances.

Activity 7: Listen, Read & Select

On Monday, two high school boys, Alex and Ben, lingered beside the trophy cabinet, discussing their soccer tournament victory the previous week.

“I keep replaying your winning goal,” Alex said. “Never have I felt such relief on a soccer field.”

“Everyone remembers the finish,” Ben replied, “but nobody mentions your interception. Had you hesitated, their striker would have scored instead.”

Alex shrugged. “At halftime, I thought we were finished. We were trailing, arguing, and trying to impress the spectators rather than support each other.”

“Only when we stopped blaming one another did we regain control,” Ben said. “Moving you into midfield gave us balance, although it meant sacrificing my chances of scoring.”

“And you accepted that without complaining,” Alex observed. “I used to think leadership meant demanding the ball. Apparently, it sometimes means creating opportunities for someone else.”

Ben glanced at the trophy. “Not only did we win the tournament, but we also learned how fragile confidence can be. Next time, we might face opponents who anticipate that formation.”

“So we shouldn’t treat one successful adjustment as a permanent solution,” Alex replied. “We need alternatives, not just another celebration.”

“Exactly. The medal proves what happened last week, not what we deserve next season.”

They headed towards class, proud of their achievement but aware that genuine progress required both ambition and humility.
